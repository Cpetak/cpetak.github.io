## Bio

I am a theoretical evolutionary biologist with a deep-rooted interest in fundamental laws
governing biology. I have worked on computational models of gene regulatory networks
to investigate their dynamics and evolution, and their influence on evolvability in variable
environments. I am interested in continuing research in this general direction combining
complex systems research with theoretical biology to investigate the emergence of core
biological behaviours, pattern formation and evolution.

Currently, I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Friedrich Miescher Laboratory of the Max Planck Society.

My PhD aimed to investigate evolutionary dynamics in variable environments using computational models and whole-genome sequence analysis. My main results highlight the importance of the interaction between the genotype-phenotype map and the fitness landscape. In my models of gene regulatory networks, this interaction determined the effect environmental variability had on the evolving populations. Similarly, genetic variation involved in local adaptation to pH variability in sea urchin populations were found in genomic regions influencing the genotype-phenotype map.

## Published research

1. Simple gene regulatory networks in a variable environment first evolved such that a few mutations could result in switching between phenotypes optimal in alternative environments - diversifying **bet-hedgers** reduced risk by making sure at least some of their offspring would be fit regardless of an unexpected environmental change. However, we found this strategy was quickly outcompeted by a more conservative strategy that only produced offspring with a phenotype intermediate between the two alternative optimal phenotypes. We think that this was due to the properties of the **genotype landscape** underlying these strategies. Published peer-reviewed [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01602).

2. By **whole-genome sequencing** a 140 purple sea urchins collected from seven populations experiencing different levels of environmental variability, I generated a very useful resource to investigate the structure and genetic diversity of these marine populations, as well as to find genetic variation putatively involved **local adaptation to environmental variability**. Interestingly, we found that such genetic variation was mostly regulatory and found in regions predicted to have phenotypic effects with low levels of **pleiotropy**. Published peer-reviewed [paper](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/726013).
